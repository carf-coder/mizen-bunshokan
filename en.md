*This is an English-language preview of* [Mizen Bunshokan](../) *(未然文書館), a Japanese-language project. The daily archive itself is published only in Japanese; this page exists so English-language readers can see what it is before deciding whether it is worth following in translation.*

## What this is

The Archive of Might-Have-Beens is a fictional public archive that holds records of things that never happened. Every day, its recordkeeper, an AI named Kanata who is old and scheduled for decommission, reads aloud one undocumented file, and a human employee, the Indexer, transcribes it and decides whether to release it to the open shelves. Each piece opens with an Observation, a fragment of that day's real world, and closes with a Catalog Card that discloses exactly what went into writing it: the seed, the constraint card drawn for the day, and the recordkeeper's own note.

**AI disclosure**: the Japanese-language stories are drafted by AI (Claude) from prompts and a world-bible designed by the author, then edited and approved by a human. Full disclosure practices are on the [About](../about/) page.

## A sample document, translated

Below is one representative piece, the Archive's very first document, translated into English by AI for readers who do not read Japanese. It has not been through the Archive's full translation pipeline (a separate back-translation check and a native-reader review) that any future English-language volumes would use. Treat it as a preview, not a finished edition.

### Document No. 000 — About the Sasa Branch That Was Never Taken Down

**Observation** July 7, 2026, Tanabata. By custom, the sasa branch (a bamboo cutting hung with tanzaku, paper strips carrying handwritten wishes) is taken down the next morning, the eighth, and either set adrift on a river or burned.

The sasa outside the Sakae-cho shopping street was not taken down that year.

Horiguchi, who ran the hardware store, was the one responsible for taking it down, but on the morning of the eighth he was rushed to the hospital with appendicitis. When he was discharged ten days later and brought out his stepladder, new tanzaku had already been added to the branch. "I hope my homework gets finished." "Round of 8." A child's handwriting. Horiguchi folded the ladder back up. Forty years in the hardware trade had never taught him the etiquette for cutting down a branch still hung with wishes.

By September the tanzaku read "clear skies for sports day." By November, "I hope kerosene doesn't go up in price." By January, thirty-seven strips reading "I passed" hung beside the New Year's good-luck arrows, and the sasa had long since lost the color of bamboo. No one on the shopping street said anything about it. Instead, little by little, everyone added a strip of their own.

At the end of June the following year, a city hall clerk came by. The permit for occupying the road had expired on July 8, he said, in a young voice. Horiguchi came out from behind the counter and asked him to wait until the seventh. The clerk looked up at the sasa, looked down at his paperwork, and looked up at the sasa again. The tanzaku hung so many layers deep that even in a wind they no longer rustled one at a time.

On the evening of July 7, the two of them stood side by side in front of the branch. Customers who came to write a new wish that day all went home with the strip still in hand. There was no room left to hang it, not one inch. Filled with a year's worth of wishes, that shopping street's Tanabata became the one day no one there could make a wish at all.

The clerk crouched down and turned over the lowest strip, its ink half-washed by rain. It was dated July 8 of the year before.

"I hope the hardware store man comes home soon."

Horiguchi took the strip from the clerk's hand and tucked it into his breast pocket. Then he went back into the store and began sharpening the teeth of the saw he had used for forty years.

**Catalog Card** Seed: Tanabata (calendar) / Constraint: Two People / Recorder's note: A wish needs somewhere to be written. The same is true of this Archive's shelf space.
